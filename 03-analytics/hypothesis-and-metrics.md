- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** The Overwhelmed veteran: a long-tenured, heavy viewer who has been with the platform for over a decade and once saw it as her default evening entertainment.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** To open the app and confidently find something worth her evening, without having to search for it.

Unlike endless scrolling or algorithmic suggestions, it offers a point of view they can trust. For StreamLine, it retains our most valuable subscribers and turns a library we already own into a reason to stay.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** She opens the app, scrolls for about twenty minutes, and closes it without watching anything. She ends up putting on a DVD instead. The catalog’s size, which should be a reason to stay, has become a reason to give up. Marcus adds the trust dimension: the recommendations treat him as a genre (“I’m not a genre, I’m a person”), so he has stopped believing the app can surface anything good.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Only 11% of homepage visitors reach a 30+ minute session, down from 19% six months ago; 71% browse but only 29% start playing. Wanderers (41% of the base) exposed to Spotlight churn 22 points less than non-exposed Wanderers, and Spotlight cohorts retain 74–79% at Month 1 vs 63–68% for Full Library.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** The Overwhelmed Veteran: a long-tenured subscriber who once used StreamLine as her default evening entertainment but now behaves like a Wanderer. Her goal is to open the app and confidently find something worth her evening. Confirmed friction: she browses without starting anything, and when she does start something, she doesn’t trust it enough to stay.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Lapsed heavy viewers can’t find a title they trust enough to start and finish, so their visits end in scrolling instead of watching.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Wanderers move from browsing to watching full sessions, which rebuilds their habit (sessions/week up from 1.1×), reduces churn, and protects the lifetime value of a segment that makes up 41% of our base.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Share of Wanderer sessions reaching 30+ minutes after engaging with Spotlight. It’s a leading indicator that moves before churn does. Month 1 retention is the lagging confirmation.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Power User sessions/week (currently 4.8×) and churn. Power Users already watch 58% curated content, and Spotlight must not cannibalize or disrupt the segment that already works.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Two more monthly cohorts (March and April) observed through Month 2. Proceed to scale if Spotlight-exposed Wanderers sustain at least a 10-point Month 1 retention lift over Full Library and the 30+ minute session rate recovers to at least 15%. Pivot if retention lifts but session depth doesn’t, and kill if neither moves.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on our persona research, which shows long-tenured viewers scrolling for 20 minutes and closing the app without watching, and funnel data showing only 11% of homepage visitors reach a 30+ minute session (down from 19%), I believe that helping viewers quickly find a title they trust enough to start and finish for the Overwhelmed Veteran, a lapsed heavy viewer now behaving like a Wanderer (41% of our base), will result in deeper viewing sessions, a rebuilt watching habit, and lower churn, as measured by a 4-point (≈36%) increase in the share of Wanderer sessions reaching 30+ minutes after engaging with Spotlight (11% → 15%). I will protect Power User engagement (4.8 sessions/week) and churn and will make a go/no-go decision after two more monthly cohorts (March and April) observed through Month 2, requiring at least a 10-point Month 1 retention lift over Full Library cohorts to proceed.
