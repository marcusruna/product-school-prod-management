# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The Overwhelmed veteran: a long-tenured, heavy viewer who has been with the platform for over a decade and once saw it as her default evening entertainment.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To open the app and confidently find something worth her evening, without having to search for it.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** She opens the app, scrolls for about twenty minutes, and closes it without watching anything. She ends up putting on a DVD instead. The catalog’s size, which should be a reason to stay, has become a reason to give up. Marcus adds the trust dimension: the recommendations treat him as a genre (“I’m not a genre, I’m a person”), so he has stopped believing the app can surface anything good.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Retreat to owned media: DVD Player
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Opens the app with the intent to watch something new.
Scrolls the home rows for about twenty minutes.
Finds nothing she trusts enough to commit to.
Closes the app.
Puts on a DVD she already owns.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** When the user cannot land on a movie to watch. The user feels its most broken due to the algorithm failing them and they decide not to continue wasting their time so they result to a choice they've already made before (DVD).
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** "I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything. There's 15,000 titles but nothing I actually want. I ended up going back to a DVD."
- **Your journey map, a shareable link, or the map file you committed (e.g. journey-map.html):** Arrive: “Tonight’s Spotlight”
User Action: Opens the app to three hand-picked films → decides in minutes, not twenty.
Internal State: Sees a short, finite list → relief replaces dread of endless scrolling.
Pain Point Addressed: Scrolling that ends with a DVD → a curated shortlist removes choice overload.
Trust: “The Curator’s Note”
User Action: Reads a short curator note on why this film → understands the choice.
Internal State: Feels recommended to as a person → trust in the platform starts rebuilding.
Pain Point Addressed: Franchise-clone recommendations → human reasoning replaces genre-matching.
Commit: “Play or Save”
User Action: Plays now or saves for later → the pick follows her to any device.
Internal State: Commits without second-guessing → confidence replaces decision fatigue.
Pain Point Addressed: Picks lost between phone and TV → her choice survives the commute home.
Return: “Next Week’s Spotlight”
User Action: Finishes the film and sees next week’s date → Spotlight becomes a ritual.
Internal State: Satisfied and anticipating → the app is her default evening again.
Pain Point Addressed: Repeated failed sessions → a weekly cadence rebuilds habit before a competitor does.
